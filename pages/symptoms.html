UNDERSTANDING ENDOMETRIOSIS

Symptoms

Endometriosis can affect people differently. Understanding possible symptoms can help you describe your experience when seeking care.

Common symptoms

Symptoms vary from person to person. Some people experience several symptoms, while others may experience only a few.

01

Severe period pain

Menstrual pain that is severe, persistent or significantly interferes with everyday activities may warrant medical evaluation.

02

Pelvic pain

Some people experience pelvic pain that occurs during menstruation or at other times during the cycle.

03

Pain during sex

Pain during or after sexual intercourse can occur and may be important to discuss with a healthcare professional.

04

Bowel symptoms

Some people may experience bowel-related symptoms, particularly around menstruation.

05

Bladder symptoms

Urinary symptoms may occur in some people and should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

06

Fatigue

Persistent fatigue can accompany chronic pain and other symptoms and may affect everyday activities.

Your experience matters.

The severity and combination of symptoms can vary considerably. Having one or more of these symptoms does not by itself mean that you have endometriosis.

Track what you're experiencing

Keeping a record of symptoms can help you communicate clearly during a healthcare appointment.

When?

Record when symptoms occur and whether they relate to your menstrual cycle.

How severe?

Describe how strongly the symptom affects you and whether it interrupts your daily activities.

What impact?

Note whether symptoms affect work, school, relationships, sleep or other activities.

When should I seek medical care?

If pain or other symptoms are persistent, severe, worsening or interfering with your everyday life, consider discussing them with a qualified healthcare professional.

Seek urgent medical attention if you experience symptoms that you believe may represent a medical emergency.
