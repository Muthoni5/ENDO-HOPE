TREATMENT & MANAGEMENT

Managing Endometriosis

Treatment is not one-size-fits-all. Understanding the options can help you have better conversations with your healthcare team.

Is there a cure?

There is currently no treatment that definitively cures endometriosis. Treatment generally focuses on managing symptoms, improving quality of life and addressing individual needs.

Endometriosis can also recur or symptoms can return after treatment, so ongoing follow-up may be part of long-term care.

Treatment should be individualized.

The right approach can depend on symptoms, treatment goals, possible side effects, long-term safety, cost, availability and whether pregnancy is desired.

Treatment options

A healthcare professional can help determine which options may be appropriate for your individual situation.

01

Pain management

Medicines may be used to help manage pain associated with endometriosis. The appropriate medicine depends on the individual and should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

02

Hormonal treatment

Hormonal medicines may help reduce endometriosis-associated pain for some people. They are not suitable for everyone and some options may not be appropriate when pregnancy is desired.

03

Surgery

Surgery may be considered in some circumstances to remove endometriosis lesions, adhesions or other affected tissue. The decision requires individual clinical assessment.

04

Fertility care

Endometriosis can be associated with difficulties becoming pregnant. People experiencing fertility concerns may discuss fertility options with an appropriate healthcare professional.

05

Non-medical support

Depending on individual needs, supportive approaches such as physiotherapy, psychological support and other multidisciplinary care may form part of a management plan.

06

Follow-up care

Endometriosis is a chronic condition. Follow-up can help healthcare professionals assess symptoms, treatment response and changing priorities over time.

What if I cannot afford treatment?

Access to diagnosis, medicines, specialist care and surgery can be difficult. Cost and availability can influence the choices available to someone seeking care.

This is one of the realities ENDO HOPE wants to address. We want to help women find reliable information about available care and resources without pretending that access is the same for everyone.

About medication

Medicines used in endometriosis care can have different purposes, benefits, risks and side effects. A medicine that is appropriate for one person may not be appropriate for another.

ENDO HOPE will provide educational information about medications, but we will not tell users what medication or dosage they personally should take.

Please do not self-medicate based on this page.

Talk to a qualified healthcare professional or pharmacist about medicines, possible interactions, side effects and whether a treatment is appropriate for you.

Questions to discuss with your healthcare provider

02 What are the possible benefits and side effects?

03 How might treatment affect fertility or pregnancy plans?

04 How much might the treatment cost?

05 What alternatives are available?

06 What should I do if my symptoms return?
